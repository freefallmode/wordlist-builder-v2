# Design: synonym groups, word forms and regional variants

Written 2026-10-31 after a discussion about `swede` appearing in "root vegetables" without `rutabaga`. Status: partly implemented (updated 2026-11-01). Numbers in sections 1 and 7 are from library v201 (62,786 entries, 58,551 roots, 2,485 playable themes). Implementation state is in section 10.

## 1. Problem

`alias` is doing three different jobs, and one of them does not fit:

| Kind today | Count | What it really is | Theme-dependent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `spelling` (colour/color) | 566 | the same word spelled differently | no |
| `form` (rainy for rain) | 1,248 | the same word in another grammatical form | no |
| `synonym` | 2,304 | a different word with (nearly) the same meaning | **yes** |
| stray kinds (`short form`, `slang`, `abbreviation`, `variant`, `"2"`...) | about 110 | mixed | mixed |

Synonyms are the problem. Whether `pudding` and `dessert` mean the same depends on the theme, so a global alias cannot be right everywhere. In practice 2,142 aliases already carry their own theme links (5,926 links): the alias is being used as a free-standing member of a theme, which is how `castle` (alias of `rook`) ended up in `buildings`, and how `swede` ended up alone in "root vegetables" while `rutabaga` was missing.

About 780 of the synonyms are really **regional words** (`flat` for `apartment`, `bonnet` for `hood`). These are different: the equivalence holds in every theme, so they do not have the problem.

## 2. Principle

> **Global** facts are about the *same word*: its forms, spellings, abbreviations and regional equivalents. **Theme-local** facts are about *different words*: synonyms.

## 3. The model

### 3.1 Entries and aliases

An entry is still one word in one sense (text plus meaning label). A root is the **default-dialect** word (US, see 3.4). Aliases are surface variants of the same word. There are six kinds:

| Kind | Example | Inherits theme links | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| `spelling` | `colour` for `color` | yes | takes the root's recognisability and typicality |
| `form` | `rainy`, `raining` for `rain` | yes | own recognisability, shared typicality (see 3.3) |
| `regional` (new) | `bonnet` for `hood`, `flat` for `apartment` | yes | own recognisability |
| `abbreviation` (new, replaces `short form`, `acronym`, `nickname`) | `PC` for `personal computer` | yes | own recognisability, shared typicality |
| `translation` (new, 2026-11-01) | `Danmark` for `Denmark`, `Roma` for `Rome`, `Gaeilge` for `Irish` | yes | own recognisability |
| `synonym` (strict only, see below) | `white mushroom` for `button mushroom` | yes | own recognisability |

A `synonym` alias is a **strict** synonym only: a word that is always the same answer as its root and can always substitute for it (same answer test, 3.3); the game may show whichever of the two suits the level difficulty. Near matches (`sofa`/`couch`) are separate entries with a synonym group. A regional, synonym, abbreviation or translation alias **may have theme links of its own** (decided 2026-11-04, see 3.5); a spelling or form alias never does. An alias with no link of its own appears in every theme its root is in. `swede` and `rutabaga` become two separate entries (or a `regional` pair, since `swede` is the UK word).

A regional variant carries `region` tags (UK, AU...). Spelling aliases carry US or UK only.

### 3.2 Synonym groups (theme-local)

A theme can declare groups of its own entries as interchangeable:

```
theme "desserts": groups: [[pudding, dessert]]
```

- Members are ordinary entries with their own ratings and theme links.
- **Rule for the game: at most one member of a group per level.**
- Another theme can list `pudding` and `dessert` as unrelated entries, or as a different group.
- A theme whose point is synonyms ("words for happy") simply declares no groups, so its members can appear together.

**Across themes.** If `pudding` (desserts) and `dessert` (meal courses) land in one level, the player is confused. This is handled by the **level generator**, not the library: the export gives every entry a `clash` id, built from the union of all theme groups it appears in, and the generator never puts two entries with the same clash id in a level. A report lists cross-theme clashes for review, because a few will be wrong where a theme treats the pair as different senses; exceptions can be added.

### 3.3 Word forms

A root is a **meaning** (`rain`), and its `form` aliases are its grammatical forms (`rainy` adjective, `raining` verb). `wind` (weather), `wind` (to turn) and `winding` are different meanings and different entries.

- **Per theme, one form is displayed.** The root's theme link may name an alias to use: `use: { weather: <id of "rainy"> }`. Default is the root itself. A theme link's typicality is shared by all forms of the entry.
- **Queryable.** A tool or a game theme can ask for the noun forms of a theme instead (`sun`, `rain`, `cloud`), which makes theme sets with matching parts of speech possible.
- Each form has its own part of speech and its own recognisability (`rainy` is not as well known as `rain`).
- **Same answer test.** A form counts as the same meaning if a player would accept it as the same answer. Derived words that have drifted (`sunny`, meaning cheerful) are a separate entry.

**Generated forms.** Agents may add a missing form where a theme prefers a part of speech, under these rules:
- it must be a normal dictionary word in everyday use, and the agent must be able to give a natural example sentence;
- it is checked against the word-frequency list, and rare forms are flagged;
- "no suitable form" is an explicit allowed answer;
- new forms stay `status: gen` until reviewed.

### 3.4 Regional variants and the default dialect

- Roots are the **US/neutral** word. Untagged means "the same everywhere".
- The regional alias carries its tags. `region` tags stay fully detailed in the data (UK, AU, NZ, IE, IN, CA, ZA and others). **No tag is merged or removed.**
- **In the game**, the player's region setting picks the member to show: the regional alias if the player's region is in its tags, otherwise the root. Only one is ever shown, so no clash rule is needed. Bucketing (for example US / UK / other, with AU, NZ, IE, IN and ZA following UK unless a word says otherwise) happens in the game, not in the data.
- **In the web app**, a setting "default dialect" (US or UK) chooses which member the app shows in lists and uses as the export fallback. It does **not** move data: with default UK the app shows `flat` for the `apartment` set. Flipping roots in the data is never needed.
- Spelling aliases: US and UK are enough.
- **Spelling pairs (library v338-v339).** The root is the US spelling and the British one is a spelling alias tagged UK. When both spellings are standard in the US (doughnut/donut, whisky/whiskey) the usual one stays root and the other has no tag. Pairs that are not really spellings (hi-fi/music center, tadpole/pollywog) are regional aliases. The validator warns (`alias-us-root`) when a spelling alias is tagged US under a non-US root. Spelling-sensitive themes keep their own word (plough in `ough_words`).
- **Rules in the game Word Balloon Safari** (its own level generator; recorded here so the data stays compatible):
  1. British players see a UK-tagged spelling or regional alias, otherwise the root.
  2. American players see a US-tagged *spelling* alias, otherwise the root. US regional-word aliases and the aliases of other regions (AU, NZ, IE, IN, CA and so on) are never shown by region.
  3. Wordplay and compound themes show exactly their linked words, as locked themes do (all such themes are locked in v339 anyway).

### 3.5 Alias families in themes, and locked themes (2026-11-04)

A root and all its aliases form an **alias family**: one meaning, one primary (the root).

- **Linking.** Any regional, synonym, abbreviation or translation member of a family may be linked in a theme on its own, with its own link rating (difficulty is then `max(its own fam, the link)`, so `handbag` is no longer as hard as `pocketbook`). Spelling and form aliases are not linked on their own; the dialect swap handles them.
- **One per theme.** At most one member of a family may be linked in a theme (validation `family-twice`). The link says which word the theme wants; the rest of the family follows.
- **One per level.** The game never puts two words of the same family in one level. The export gives each word its family (the root), as it already does in the root column.
- **Locked themes.** A theme is *locked* when the game must show exactly the linked word. It then does not swap in a regional or spelling variant or another family member. Locked by default when the theme kind is `wordplay` or `compound`, or the theme (or an ancestor) is language-specific (`lang`); a theme can override either way with `locked: true` or `false` (theme dialog, Word substitution). The export writes bit 64 (`locked`) in the theme flags.
- **Unlocked themes** swap by the player's region as in 3.4: if the linked word has a family member for the player's region, that member is shown. A UK-only member linked in an unlocked theme is shown as such to a UK player, and swapped to the US word for a US player.
- `show` is separate: it is the short display text of an entry in a theme (`fly` for `greenfly` in a compound theme). It stays and works with locked and unlocked themes.

### 3.6 Compound and modifier members: the entry is the full term (2026-11-05)

What the player is asked to recognise is the **full term**, so the entry, its ratings, part of speech, type and flags describe the full term, never the bare word.

- **Compound themes** (`tkind` compound, rule `compound-end:room` or `compound-start:fire`): each member is an entry for the full term (`break room`, `firefly`); the theme shows the short word through `show` (`break`, `fly`). The entry carries `nameOkIn` for the theme, since the full term contains the theme's shared word. `fam`, the link rating, pos, type, adult, sensitive, tm, vulgar and region are judged for the full term (a harmless word can make a sensitive term: `crack house`). Loose phrases (an ordinary word plus the shared word: `good time`) are not members; only fixed terms are.
- **Kinds-of-X themes with modifier members:** a member that is only a modifier and does not mean the thing without its head noun (`green`, `herbal`, `bubble` in teas; `mute` in swans; `igneous` in rocks; `solid` in line types; `prime` in number types) is the full-term entry (`green tea`) shown as the modifier. A bare word that already means the thing (`pine`, `tote`, `leatherback`, `Pacific`, a saint's first name) stays as it is.
- **Kinds themes: the test for the short display (2026-11-06).** The question is whether the short form, seen alone in the theme, still identifies the kind.
  - *Names the thing alone* stays bare: breed and variety names people say alone (`merino`, `Gala`, `Siberian`, `Newfoundland`), genre and material names (`axe`, `bomba`, `veneer`), sibling notations (`9-volt`, `AA`).
  - *Modifier only* is the full term shown short: `oat milk` shown as "oat", `single room` as "single", `cuckoo clock` as "cuckoo". This covers bare modifiers entered as the wrong word (the grain, not the milk) and full terms still shown in full (`powdered milk`, `sports car`, `mantel clock`).
  - *Closed compounds* (`buttermilk`, `redwood`, `sailboat`) and *hyphenated terms* (`A-road`, `post-rock`, `half-elf`) are never split; the game may split them later. They carry `nameOkIn` where they contain the theme word.
  - *Head first, names and fixed phrases* stay whole (`island chain`, `cloud bank`, `massage gun`, `Google Maps`, `Lady Apple`). A head-first phrase for the same thing as a modifier-first one is a synonym alias of it (`milk powder` of `powdered milk`).
  - *Borderline* stays whole: when the short word could as easily point to another kind (`town car`, `toy train`, `tiny house`, `city car`, `night train`).
  - Done in the library (v283 to v288): hotel room types by hand; 36 + 156 bare modifiers made full terms (library-wide, 22 themes breed lines excluded); 39 full terms given a short display; `powdered milk`. Tools: `tools/gen/bare-sample.mjs`, `BRIEF-bare.md`, `bare-apply.mjs`; `full-list.mjs`, `BRIEF-full.md`, `full-apply.mjs`. The same sorting applies to new entries in kinds themes (js/ai.js).
- **Entries with the same text** keep their meaning labels apart (`overlook` the verb and the viewpoint); a word that exists already is reused, not duplicated.
- **Shown text** repeats across entries more often now (several members show `board`); the generator treats two words with the same shown text as clashing in one level, as for synonym groups.
- Done in the library: all compound themes (v274 pilot, then the other 48 in v275: 130 links dropped, 923 moved to existing full terms, 703 new full-term entries, 394 emptied bare words deleted). Tools: `tools/gen/compound-make.mjs`, `BRIEF-compound.md`, `compound-apply.mjs`; modifiers: `BRIEF-modifier.md`, `compound-apply.mjs --prefix m`.

## 4. Splitting themes

Many themes can yield new themes of 4 or more words, especially once forms are queryable (`weather`: 58 nouns and 8 adjectives could give a `weather conditions` theme of adjectives and a `rough weather` theme of storms). Rules:
- A split theme should have at least 4 distinct words (relaxed from 8, 2026-11-03), be a real subtype or a clear membership test (not just a shared word: "water" in drinks is not a subtype), and have a name that does not leak its words.
- **Overlap rule** (2026-11-03). A theme must not list two words for the same thing, or a word and a more specific form of it, unless they are grouped. Cola and Coca-Cola, or pasta and pasta sauce, are the mistake. Fixes, in order: (1) the same thing under two names becomes one entry (the shortest form; the other an alias or dropped); (2) a brand and the generic word it stands for (cola and Coca-Cola): in a theme of a general category (soft drinks) they are one answer, so they go in a synonym group, or the brand leaves the theme when it adds nothing; in a theme of examples or kinds (fizzy drinks) Pepsi, cola, Sprite and lemonade are separate members and all stay. Test: would a player hesitate over which of the two a word is? A brand with no generic word listed in the theme stays; (3) four or more subtypes of one thing become their own sub-theme; (4) fewer than four stay in the parent and go in a theme-local synonym group so a level never uses both. The validator reports candidates as `overlap` (info).
- **A member is one of the theme's things, not a mix of them** (2026-11-03). A word that stands for a mixture, collection or the whole theme does not belong in it: `mixed nuts` is not a kind of nut (removed from `nuts`), and the same goes for `assorted sweets` in a sweets theme, `fruit salad` in a fruits theme or `cutlery` in a theme of knives and forks. It can sit in a theme of its own kind (snacks, dishes). The same holds for the bare category word in a theme named for its kinds ("types of X", "X types", breeds, varieties, species, styles): `road` is not a type of road, `aircraft` is not an aircraft type (7 such links removed from kebab, road, settlement, aircraft, motorcycle, traveler and soldier themes). The exception for the bare word of a plural-named theme (`snake` in snakes) does not apply there. Generation prompts and reviewers apply this as part of the overlap check.
- **Complete themes** (131 complete themes at v374, each with a note that lists the exact members) are only split into pieces that are themselves exact sets.
- A word may sit in a parent and a child, but the level generator must not use the parent and its split-off children together. Check how the game handles sibling overlap before adding many splits.
- Splits are proposed by agents, names approved by a person.

## 5. Ratings

Terms (2026-11-03). **Familiarity** (`fam`): how well known the word is, on its own. **Typicality** (`typ[theme]`, `typicality` in prompts and data modules): how readily the word comes to mind for one theme. **Word difficulty** (revised 2026-11-05): `0.7 x the harder + 0.3 x the easier` of the two ratings for a word in a theme, one decimal from 1.0 to 5.0 (the single rating alone when only one is set, 0 when neither); computed from the stored ratings and exported per link as the decimal `difficulty` (the whole-number "harder of the two", `difficultyBand`, remains only for counting and filtering words by band in the editor). **Theme popularity** (`pop`): how familiar and appealing the theme is for a general player. Stored and code names match the terms since 2026-11-06 (entry `rec` became `fam`, entry `th` became `typ`, link `popularity` in the export became `typicality`, the export column `rec` became `familiarity`; `popIn` is `typicality()`, `effRec` is `difficultyBand()`); theme `pop` and `ident` are unchanged. Data modules in tools/gen keep the old `inRec` and `rec` words in their comments and rows.

Theme identifiability (2026-11-05, `ident` on a theme, 1 obvious to 5 obscure, 0 unrated): how easily a player works out the theme from four of its members, whatever the difficulty of those words. Wordplay themes whose mechanic is hard to see (hidden words, letter changes) and broad themes ("things made of metal") rate high even when every word is easy. It is judged from the theme alone, not from the words the game will pick, so it is independent of word difficulty. **Theme difficulty** is `ident + 0.5 x (pop - 3)`, kept between 1.0 and 5.0, one decimal: identifiability sets the level and popularity moves it by at most one step (a very popular theme is easier, an unpopular one harder). It is 0 where `ident` is unrated, and `ident` alone where `pop` is. It is never stored (computed when needed) and exported as the decimal `difficulty` theme column. Identifiability was rated for every theme in two blind passes by agents (tools/gen/ident-make.mjs, BRIEF-ident.md, ident-compare.mjs); the second pass, with midpoints where the passes differed by two or more, is the stored rating. The game selects themes by theme difficulty and then words by word difficulty; sibling-theme overlap and enough words at a level are checks, not ratings. Only the decimal values are exported for difficulty (the game reads them, `popularity` stays as the fallback for an unrated theme).

Unchanged in meaning (`fam`: how well known the word is; theme link `typ`: how typical it is of the theme), applied as in the table in 3.1. A regional variant's `fam` is judged for the audience of its region.

## 6. Export (format version 3)

- Aliases keep the root pointer, with kind bits for spelling, form, regional, abbreviation and translation (16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512 for a strict synonym).
- Each alias row has its part of speech and region mask.
- Each theme link may have a `use` form index.
- Each entry gets a `clash` id (3.2).
- The game repo has not been updated to format 2 yet, so it can be moved straight to 3.

## 7. Migration plan

Run on a prototype slice first (`weather`, plus about ten synonym-heavy themes), then the library. Each phase ends with checks and a commit.

| Phase | Work | Size | Who |
|---|---|---|---|

| 1 | Spelling: tag every spelling alias US or UK | 311 untagged | script (by spelling pattern) plus review |
| 2 | Regional words: `synonym` with a differing region becomes `regional`; flip the roots that are the UK word | 779 pairs; about 105 sure flips (UK root, US alias), plus some of 243 where the root is untagged but is really the UK word | script for the sure cases, agents for the 243 |
| 3 | Remaining synonyms (about 1,500): each becomes an independent entry. In each theme where the root and the synonym are both present they become a group; a synonym that only has its own link becomes a plain entry; a link that was really a different sense gets its own entry with a label | about 1,500 | agents, by theme |
| 4 | Families: merge `rain`/`rainy` pairs inside themes, then give forms parts of speech and a `use` form per theme | 1,248 forms plus in-theme duplicates | script plus agents |
| 5 | Split themes | open ended | agents propose, person approves |
| 6 | Export v3 and the game | | |

Safeguards: validation rules for each kind, a count of aliases with theme links (target 0), and a before/after list of every theme's member set so no theme loses words by accident.

## 8. Open questions

All three are answered. One member per group per level holds for every theme with groups (3.2). The game does its own region bucketing from the export's region masks (section 10, 2026-11-03). The game reads export format 3 (library v374 pinned, ISSUES.md), including the root pointer and the alias kind bits.

## 9. Shortened text in a theme (`show`)

Added 2026-11-01. A theme may show an entry under a shorter text than its own: `rock garden` is shown as `rock` in "garden styles" (`show: {garden_styles: "rock"}`). This is a theme-local fact, so it is **not** an alias: `rock` does not mean "rock garden" in any other theme. The earlier kept-by-name review recorded these as `form` aliases of the bare word; 1,147 of them were turned back into entries of their own (`tools/gen/show-migrate.mjs`). `form` now means a grammatical form only. Like `use`, `show` is written by export format 3 (see section 8).

## 10. Decisions log and implementation state

This section is a dated log: each entry records what was decided or done at the time, and a later entry can change an earlier one. **Rules in force** below collect the rules that still apply, with the entry that states each; where an entry is marked *superseded* it is kept only as history. Field and format reference: DESIGN.md.

**Rules in force**
- A theme shows a word through `use` (a form) or `show` (a shorter text); the entry keeps its identity, ratings and links. Shortening is not aliasing. Two words that display the same in a theme are allowed in the library; the export keeps the more popular.
- A theme name must describe its members by itself, outside the tree, and carries its own capitals only for proper nouns and acronyms. The theme-name check uses the shown text.
- A member is the full term (3.6); containing a member's word does not make an entry a member (overlap rule). Bare short members are a `show` on the full term.
- `synonym` is an alias kind for a strict synonym only (the same answer as its root); near matches are separate entries in a theme synonym group (3.1, 3.2). Aliases of the kinds regional, synonym, abbreviation and translation may hold links of their own (3.5).
- Roots are the US or neutral word and every region tag is kept. A theme's `region` and a word's `region` mean who knows it; ratings (`fam`, `typ`, `pop`, `ident`) are always for a general US/UK adult.
- Theme difficulty is never stored; it is computed from `ident` and `pop`. A complete theme has a note listing its exact members.
- Capitals: a word is shown the way it is written for its sense; the rule and the lint are in "Capital rule and lint" below. A case-only shown text is not used in wordplay or compound themes, nor on an entry that has regional or spelling aliases.
- Export additions are additive within format 3; the field list is DESIGN.md section 8.

**Log**

Decisions taken while building the weather prototype (2026-11-01), with the reason. The rest of this file is the plan they refine.

**Decisions**
- **Synonym groups are theme-local.** `theme.groups` is a list of lists of entry ids; a word is in one group per theme. Only one member of a group appears in a level, except in word-puzzle themes that declare no groups. The export gives every word in any group a `clash` id (groups joined across themes); the level builder never puts two with one id in a level.
- **Forms are grammatical only.** rain, rainy and raining are one meaning. The 1,164 "form" aliases that were really compounds or full names (rock garden of rock, school uniform of uniform, storm cloud of storm) were wrong: they came from a text-matching rule in the kept-by-name review script. They were rebuilt as entries (section 9). `hot springs`, `skis` and similar stay forms.
- **A theme shows a word through `use` (a form) or `show` (a shorter text).** The entry keeps its identity, ratings and links; the display is a property of the theme link. A form's own recognisability counts when it is shown (the harder of the two). The part of speech shown is the form's, so a theme of adjectives can be built from noun roots.
- **Shortening is not aliasing.** A bare word is a valid alias only where it means the same thing everywhere. Where the category supplies the meaning ("what type of garden? rock") it is `show`.
- **Duplicates in display are allowed in the library**; the export keeps the more popular (then the easier) and leaves the other out of that theme. If an obvious second name exists (electric ray -> torpedo) it is used instead.
- **Theme-name check uses the shown text**, so rock garden is fine where it shows as rock.
- **Roots are the US/neutral word; every region tag is kept** (applied in phases 1-2). The web-app default-dialect setting only changes what is shown and the export fallback; it never moves data.
- **Region in the game** is chosen from the player's setting, never by a clash rule: the regional alias if the player's region is in its tags, else the root.
- **Generated forms** (misty, hazy, showery, drizzly, thundery) follow section 3.3: ordinary dictionary words with a natural sentence, status `gen` until reviewed.
- **Weather split:** `weather` is nouns only; `weather conditions` (24 adjectives) is its child; the rough-weather part already exists as `storms`.

**Style rule (v374, 2026-11-15).** After making `gothic` and `byzantine` lowercase among the art movements, Simon asked for all such names to be standardised by one rule and left the rule to the manager. The rule: **the name of a style, movement or school (art, architecture, design, literature, fashion) is lowercase. A word in it keeps a capital only when English has that word only as a name or the adjective of a name**: a person or ruler (`Victorian`, `Georgian`, `Edwardian`, `Elizabethan`, `Tudor`, `Palladian`, `Queen Anne`, `Pre-Raphaelites`), a place or people (`Greek revival`, `Norman`, `Moorish`, `Chicago school`, `Cape Cod`), an organisation (`Bauhaus`), or a word coined from a name that has no lowercase use (`Romanesque`, `Italianate`). A word that English also uses in lowercase is lowercase as a style name even if it began as a name: `gothic`, `byzantine`, `baroque`, `rococo`, `renaissance`, `regency`, `colonial`, `empire line`, `dada`, `art deco`, `art nouveau`, `beaux-arts`, `craftsman`, `perpendicular`, `international style`. Reason for drawing the line there: it is the one test that gives Simon's own decisions (`cubism`, `gothic`, `byzantine` lowercase) without lowercasing words that only ever appear as names (`Victorian`, `Moorish`); it is close to the Guardian style guide's rule for art movements and differs from the Chicago Manual, which capitalises `Gothic` and `Byzantine`. **The name of a historical period is a name**: `Renaissance`, `Baroque` and `Regency` keep their capital in themes of periods (`historical periods`, `ages of history`), as separate entries from the style. Titles and trademarks keep their own spelling (`American Gothic`, `Century Gothic`).

Applied by `tools/gen/style-case.mjs` (one line per decision; it changes nothing when the entries are right, so it can be rerun as a check): 30 entries lowercased and typed common, most of them in "architecture styles", which had every member capitalised (`Baroque`, `Brutalism`, `Modernist`, `International Style`); 3 keep a capital only on the name inside (`Chicago school`, `Greek revival`, `Tudor revival`); `pre-raphaelites` and `cape cod` became `Pre-Raphaelites` and `Cape Cod`; the link of `Renaissance` [period] to "art movements" and "art" moved to the lowercase entry `renaissance` [style], which architecture already had. The lint has a kind for it, `style-capital`: in a theme whose name ends in styles, movements or genres, a member shown with a capital whose first word is in `STYLE_WORDS` (`js/caselint-words.js`). `gothic` and `byzantine` left `PROPER_WORDS`. Not swept: style names in music and other fields (`Delta blues`, `Detroit techno` already follow the rule), and the classical orders (`Doric`, `Ionic`, `Corinthian`, `Composite`), which are still all capitalised.

**Brand types (v378, 2026-11-16).** A brand name is typed `org` (`TYPE_HELP`: company, brand) with `tm`, as in the brand themes (liqueurs, spreads, cereals, soft drinks, beers, American candy); a named product or model within a maker's range is `proper` with `tm` (`Big Mac`, `Tesla Model 3`). `tools/gen/batch378.mjs` moved 23 outliers to their theme's majority. Left as they are, consistent among themselves but `proper`: brand cheeses (`Boursin`, `Jarlsberg`), hats (`Stetson`, `Akubra`), adhesives (`Sellotape`, `Blu Tack`), and toys, games and gadgets, which are split across both types.

**Single-word names: the five rules (Simon, 2026-11-16).** They settle the calls above; v382 (`tools/gen/batch382.mjs`, `tools/gen/batch382-changes.txt`) made the library follow them.
1. **Dictionary rule, with a tie-break:** a word takes the capital its sense has in standard dictionaries (Oxford, Merriam-Webster). If they disagree, or M-W says "often capitalized", it stays lowercase (the cheddar rule): `cotija`, `klondike`, `brie`, `parmesan`, `camembert`.
2. **No dictionary entry:** follow the sibling convention in the same theme. `Choron` keeps its capital (sauces named after people are capital: `Mornay`, `sauce Robert`); `ankara` [fabric] is lowercase (place-named fabrics are: `denim`, `cashmere`, `madras`, `paisley`).
3. **Grape varieties** are lowercase unless the name contains a proper word, which keeps its capital (`Müller-Thurgau`, `nero d'Avola`). Wines named after places follow rule 9 below.
4. **Trademarks follow the sense of the theme:** a brand in its brand sense is capital, `org`, with `tm` (`Kleenex`, `Cointreau`); a genericised trademark in "brand names used as words" is lowercase, common, with `tm` kept (`xerox`, `teflon`, `formica`, `jacuzzi`, `bakelite`, `tannoy`), when rule 6 makes it lowercase.
5. **A capital follows the whole family of the sense:** `Caesar` [dressing], `Caesar salad` and `Caesar` [cocktail] are all capital; `Florentine` [of Florence] is capital because all its demonym siblings are; `florentine` [biscuit] stays lowercase.

**Single-word names: rules 6 to 9 (Simon, 2026-11-16).** v384 (`tools/gen/batch384.mjs`, `tools/gen/batch384-changes.txt`) applied the one change they made (`burgundy` [wine]).
6. **When a brand is lowercase:** a trademark is lowercase only when both dictionaries print the sense lowercase or they disagree (rule 1's tie-break). If both capitalise the noun it stays capital `org` with `tm`, even in "brand names used as words" (`Hoover`, `Kleenex`; `thermos` is lowercase because they disagree). A word also used as a verb gets a separate lowercase verb entry, as `google`; `hoover` [verb] is a candidate for a later split.
7. **What `tm` marks:** product and brand names. Company names (`Ford`, `Google` [company]) carry no `tm`. `Jeep` is a vehicle brand, so its `tm` is right; no American car brand needs one added.
8. **Root of an accent pair:** the headword form in English dictionaries. If no dictionary has the word, the original-language spelling for foreign words and names (`Rhône`, `cacık`, `çiğ köfte`); a fully naturalised word stays plain (`guarana`, `bun cha`).
9. **Place-named wines and unverified calls:** a wine named after a place is capital only when the dictionaries capitalise the sense; if both print it lowercase, or a dictionary could not be checked, it is lowercase. A call made without checking a dictionary is marked "recheck" in the change log (`muscadet` is the first). `Bordeaux` stays capital as established (a precedent; one dictionary capitalises it), flagged for Simon.

**T24 under the five rules (v383, 2026-11-16, Simon approved all six groups).** `tools/gen/batch383.mjs`; reasons per word in `tools/gen/batch383-changes.txt`. One line per decision:
